A Hitchhiker's Guide to the Distributed Adversarial Model

Based on papers I co-authored.
Beholder signatures (CRYPTO'26)
The opposite of blind signatures and threshold signatures: prove that you individually know both your secret key and what you have signed. This gives us non-interactive proofs of custody and other blockchain applications.

eprint

On the Pitfalls of Modeling Individual Knowledge (CCS'26)
In the Distributed Adversarial Model, the Multi-Party Random Oracle (MPRO) only models the compression function, and not the variable-length hash. Here, we explain what goes wrong if one assumes the latter is modeled.

eprint

Strong secret sharing with snitching (CRYPTO'25)
Secret sharing with snitching ensured security under full corruption, using blockchain smart contracts to enforce honest behavior. But what if the adversary also sets up smart contracts of their own?

eprint  ·  crypto'25 talk

Secret sharing with snitching (CCS'24)
It feels like obtaining secret sharing under full corruption is a hopeless endeavor. In this paper we show that it's actually possible, if the adversary is fragmented and might consist of multiple factions that might be mutually adversarial. As for blockchain application, this leads to a new way of MEV prevention.
