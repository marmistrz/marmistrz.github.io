My papers in a nutshell

On the Pitfalls of Modeling Individual Knowledge (CCS'26)
It is tempting to model a Merkle-Damgård hash function as a random oracle, but this may go terribly wrong if the protocol is meant to prevent collusion. We attack two papers (published at CCS'15 and CCS'24) and show how the iterative structure of the Merkle-Damgård hash can be turned into an attack.

eprint

Towards Practical Committee Sizes in YOSO MPC (CRYPTO'26)
YOSO MPC provides stellar security guarantees for MPC-on-a-blockchain, but the committee sizes remained in the several thousands: too large for practical deployments. We bring them down to the order of 100.

eprint

Beholder signatures (CRYPTO'26)
The opposite of blind signatures and threshold signatures: prove that you individually know both your secret key and what you have signed. This means that the singature may not have obliviously been computed using MPC! This gives us non-interactive proofs of custody and other blockchain applications.

eprint

The Malice of ELFs: practical anamorphic-resistant encryption without random oracles (EUROCRYPT'26)
Anamorphic encryption promises to end all crypto wars by using ciphertexts as steganographic vessels. However, this promise falls short once the dictator is allowed to choose the encryption scheme adversarially.

eprint

Strong secret sharing with snitching (CRYPTO'25)
Secret sharing with snitching ensured security under full corruption, using blockchain smart contracts to enforce honest behavior. But what if the adversary also sets up smart contracts of their own?

eprint  ·  crypto'25 talk

Secret sharing with snitching (CCS'24)
It feels like obtaining secret sharing under full corruption is a hopeless endeavor. In this paper we show that it's actually possible, if the adversary is fragmented and might consist of multiple factions that might be mutually adversarial. As for blockchain application, this leads to a new way of MEV prevention.
